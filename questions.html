Questions

Questions I keep returning to. If one of them is yours too, write to me: jf {at} janfarrell.com.

How long does the public keep access to frontier AI?

Export controls have already reached the frontier. I do not expect the window of open public access to stay open, and I think we are living in the strange period before it closes.

Can a machine be governed in ten lines?

The most durable rule set in history is ten lines long, and its power was that a breach could be named. I wonder what ten lines would mean for machines.

What do we owe the question of machine consciousness?

Care, mostly, and slower vocabulary. A grey area does not oblige attribution. I made a first attempt at the argument in a letter to the Financial Times.

Should Britain build sovereign AI capability?

Sovereignty is expensive and the odds are unkind. I do, however, feel that the United Kingdom is the one credible third seat: it has the labs, the engineers, and the convening tradition.

Should we resist the end of jobs?

A motion the Society will argue this autumn. I chair, so I argue neither side.

What is fear for?

Racing at 231.66 km/h taught me to treat fear as information rather than an enemy. I keep testing where else that holds: in companies, in investing, and in how we talk about machines. I wrote about it in “Yes, I’m scared.”

How much protein is too much?

Nutrition labels report protein but say nothing about its excess. As an athlete and a father I built a simple ratio to see it.
