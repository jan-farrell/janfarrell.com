Standing questions

Where I have reached a view on the machine questions, it is set out in Where I stand on AI.

How long does the public keep access to frontier AI?

Export controls have already reached the frontier. I do not expect the window of open public access to stay open, and I think we are living in the strange period before it closes.

Can a machine be governed in ten lines?

The most durable rule set in history is ten lines long, and its power was that a breach could be named. My current draft for machines has nine lines and one open seat, and it is open for review.

What do we owe the question of machine consciousness?

Care, mostly, and slower vocabulary. A grey area does not oblige attribution. I made a first attempt at the argument in the Financial Times.

How are we going to deal with super-persuasion?

We must brace ourselves for machines that read us so well that their output has dangerously manipulative power. I am not yet sure how we deal with that. One habit that holds: ask every model for the strongest case against what it has just told you.

Should Britain build sovereign AI capability?

Sovereignty is expensive and the odds are unkind. I do, however, feel that the United Kingdom is the one credible third seat: it has the labs, the engineers, and the convening tradition.

Should we resist the end of jobs?

The Society argued it at the House of Lords on 10 September 2026, and carried the motion that we should encourage the end of jobs, not resist it, by 21 to 15. I chaired, so I argued neither side on the night. Where I have landed since: if we fight the change we will get it wrong, and if we encourage it we might get it right. The end of jobs is not the goal in itself.

What is fear for?

Racing at 231.66 km/h taught me to treat fear as information rather than an enemy. I keep testing where else that holds: in companies, in investing, and in how we talk about machines. I wrote about it in “Yes, I’m scared.”

How much protein is too much?

Nutrition labels report protein but say nothing about its excess. As an athlete and a father I built a simple ratio to see it.
